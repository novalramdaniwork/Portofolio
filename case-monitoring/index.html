Alerts that reach the right person

The path an alert takes

  1. Prometheus checks custom rules every scrape. When, say, CPU stays above 85% for five minutes, the alert fires.
  2. Alertmanager deduplicates, groups related alerts and suppresses lower ones while a higher one is active.
  3. It posts to an n8n webhook, which reads the severity and formats the message.
  4. Critical goes to the critical channel; warnings and info go to the general one.
  5. Telegram delivers it with the service, host, current value, threshold and time.

Two channels, and commands

The critical channel carries only things that need action now: a service down, replication stopped, a crash loop, a disk above 90%. The general channel gets warnings to review during the day: CPU above 75%, replication lag, growing queues, a disk above 75%.

During an incident I want a specific answer without opening Grafana. Each command runs an n8n workflow that queries Prometheus and replies in seconds.

CommandReturns
/check_serverCPU, memory and disk for every server
/check_dbMariaDB replication status and connections
/check_k8sPod status across all clusters
/check_queueRabbitMQ queue depth and consumers
/statusA summary of everything

What I learned

Alert fatigue is the real failure

The first version fired on normal variance, and within a week real alerts were being missed. I tuned thresholds to observed baselines and deleted every rule that fired without needing action.

Two channels, not more

Splitting critical from general made the biggest difference. Per-service channels just made it unclear where to look.

Monitor the monitoring

If n8n is down, nothing reaches Telegram. A blackbox check watches it, with a direct email route from Alertmanager as the fallback.
